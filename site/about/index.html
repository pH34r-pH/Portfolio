ABOUT / TYLER J.H.G.

Curiosity needs a test bench.

I’m Tyler James Harbin-Giuntoli, a software developer at Microsoft. My independent work focuses on machine learning, research engineering, and formal methods.

I like questions that connect a model’s behavior to something I can inspect: a state, an operation, a proof, or a run record. This site follows those questions through the research and the systems that make the experiments practical.

How I work

I build machine-learning systems around questions I can test: what information a compact state preserves, how a model uses it, and how much computation the result costs.

I follow an idea through the experiment, the implementation, and the evidence. A useful explanation should make the mechanism clear enough that someone else can inspect it and try it. The articles explain the question and the comparison; the notebooks and experiment packages give the reader something to take apart.

Small examples are useful because you can see the whole mechanism at once. From there, the interesting question is what survives when the task, the data, or the available hardware changes.

What keeps me interested

Machine learning, robotics, and philosophy keep pulling me toward the same kinds of questions: what a system knows, what it can do with that information, and how we tell whether our explanation is any good.

I’m interested in what happens when an elegant idea meets a battery, a memory limit, or a part that needs repairing. Those constraints are part of the problem. They often make the next experiment more interesting.

Find my work

The research articles follow the current investigation. Compiled experiments connect code, artifacts, and run records.
